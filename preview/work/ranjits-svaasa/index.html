History is not merely written in books; it is carried forward from generation to generation.. witnessed, lived, and lovingly passed down.

In the once-quiet lanes of Amritsar’s walled city settled a family of traders who journeyed along the Silk Route, carrying spices and tea from Afghanistan and beyond to the lush gardens of Assam and Bengal.

Life was celebrated, remembered, and savored within the embrace of the Old City. Of the few families who stepped beyond its walls, the Mehra family was among the four, finding their new home on Mall Road.

Rama Kapoor

In this portrait from the Mehra family archives is Rama Kapoor on her wedding day in 1977.

Born into an aristocratic family in Dhanbad, Rama married Ranjit Rattan Mehra of Amritsar in a grand ceremony at The Ashok, New Delhi.. a woman of quiet strength and grace.

Where life changed too soon, determined to honour his memory, Rama Ranjit Mehra undertook the heartfelt endeavour of restoring her husband’s ancestral haveli, where they had lived their early married life. She transformed the haveli into Ranjit’s Svaasa, a home that offered solace, succour, service.

And today, it does exactly what she hoped for.

Ranjit
Rattan Mehra

One half of the wedding archival photograph holds the image of Ranjit Rattan Mehra, tall and dashing at 6 feet 3 inches, charismatic in an effortless way, and deeply loved by all who knew him.

Born into the lineage of Rai Bahadur Lala Ratan Chand Mehra, Ranjit grew up in a home where dignity was lived, not spoken.

His marriage to Rama Kapoor in 1977 brought together two families of heritage and warmth. Their early years were spent in his ancestral haveli on Mall Road.. the very space that would later become Ranjit’s SVAASA.

‘Svaasa’, from ‘sv-aas’, literally a breath of life.

Walled City

Across the old walled city of Amritsar, twelve historic gates once guarded a world shaped by traders, travellers, saints, & kings… each entrance a threshold that defined how the city breathed, moved, and grew.

From Hathi Gate, where elephants once passed, to Lohgarh Gate, once the armoury entrance and now a thriving market street, the old routes now hum with shops and everyday life.

Here, on this greater map of memory and movement, Ranjit’s Svaasa was once a modest chapter, before being reimagined, restored, and returned to the world as a haveli that carries forward the heritage of the city it belongs to.

Old Amritsar

Captured by Felice Beato in the late 1850s, this photograph reveals the texture of old Amritsar.. its balconies, its bustle, its interplay of shadow & sun.

Generations moved through these very lanes, carrying tales of craft, commerce, and kinship. The Mehra family lived among them, shaped quietly by the everyday rhythms of this city.

The family till date is known as the ‘Chai wale Mehras’.

The Haveli

Built roughly 200 years ago, in the mid-19th century, after the era of Maharaja Ranjit Singh, this haveli began as a residence for the Mehra family, centred around its angan, the open courtyard that anchored everyday life. Structurally, the haveli reflects the layered architectural evolution of Punjab.

Its angan, baked-earth Nanakshahi bricks, the coloured tile baked-flooring, and the lime-plastered walls represent early Punjabi building traditions.

It is a home that has carried many eras… yet still breathes with the quiet symmetry it was born with.

The SVAASA Film

Directed by AJC

The old lock opens… and with it, a world two centuries deep.

Once fitted to the original doors of the Haveli,

this Aligarh-crafted Royal lock carries the quiet memories of life within these walls... moments that shaped generations of the Mehra family. The home, once fondly known as ‘Ritz Walo Ki Kothi’, still holds echoes of its storied past.

Within this very courtyard, marriages were celebrated, children were born, and the women of the house saw milestone chapters unfold. Thus a home where a simple relic was preserved… holding echoes of a home where stories were lived long before they were ever told.

“I bow not to them, but to my lineage. I salute my ancestry, whose strength allows me to hold this position... and to use it in service of my people.”

For Rai Bahadur Lala Ratan Chand, these were not merely words, but a way of life.

A builder of Amritsar’s civic foundations, a champion of welfare, and the force behind hospitals, schools, and public institutions that still echo his generosity. His life was devoted to the people he served.

A just man. A noble legacy. Our forefather.

Four generations of strength, standing together.

O.B.E. Rai Bahadur Lala Ratan Chand Mehra with his sons: Rai Bahadur Prakash Chand Mehra, Rai Bahadur Labh Chand Mehra, Lala Khushal Chand Mehra and Lala Lal Chand Mehra.

A family that shaped the civic, cultural, and philanthropic spirit of Amritsar. Their work built institutions, uplifted communities, and laid the foundations of the city we know today.

At Ranjit’s SVAASA, their legacy lives, just as across the city of Amritsar... that was their home, their people.

Lahé Life

The strategy was to build a digital archive where a person could travel through centuries of time before returning to live in the present, which is precisely how Svaasa feels when you stay there.

Every creative choice was an intentional act of preservation. The reliance on sepia and black-and-white tones strips away modern noise, while the acoustic soundscape, sourcing the rare, nostalgic Punjabi folk tunes of Surinder Kaur and the classical depth of Bhai Bidi Chand.. restores the rhythm of the province. You may find yourself humming these tunes when you’re at Svaasa.

Narrative

Ranjit’s Svaasa is a 200-year-old palatial haveli, now a family-hosted boutique homestay in the heart of Amritsar city.

The Mehra family, who still opens these ancestral doors to the world, has roots embedded seven generations deep into this soil. When we approached the creative direction for their social media and brand film, we knew the narrative could not merely start from the present. It had to honor all those who lived before us – the lineage of the family, the civic fabric of Amritsar as shaped by them, and the timeless sanctum of the Golden Temple as respected and taken care of by them.
