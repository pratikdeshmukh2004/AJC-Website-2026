AJC in Ladakh / لداخ

Balti Kitchen

Brand Identity
Narrative
Menu Design

Ladakh’s Turtuk village was our home for the latter half of 2024. Turtuk is a lush land of greens cradled between the stark, colossal mountains of Ladakh. There, you see buckwheat fields swaying at the slightest breeze and local farmer-women at work, beautifully unbothered by the summer sun. In a parallel vision, there was I, who would often be tempted to take a walk up the mountain paths to reach the village. Knowing me, my walks invariably led me to Balti Kitchen, where the finest local cuisine in town was to be found. Run by Hajira Khan, the first female entrepreneur from the village, Balti Kitchen is a space where the ancient recipes of Baltistan live on through the day. Cooked to this day in traditional stone pots, her herbed, mellow, yet deeply flavorful food became the very birthplace of Balti Kitchen’s new identity.

Identity

Borrowed from ancient traditions, the identity is as symbolic as it is literal. Drawing from the historic kitchens and traditional utensils of Baltistan, the logo comes together beneath a Mehrab – an arch native to Islamic architecture often used on entryways. Here, it acts as a visual gateway, welcoming everyone into a world of revived ancient culinary traditions within modern-day Ladakh.

Colour Palette

Forming the color palette were the earthen pots made of mud, rock, and clay from a time when women ruled the hearth and men crafted the intricate woodwork for which the region is famous. Because the natural hue of mud coincides beautifully with the deep tones of oak and native timber, the visual inspiration came together seamlessly – paying homage to the collective ancestral skills that brought Balti Kitchen to the world.

Menu Design

The menu came through with many conversations and some very filling meals. The descriptions were key for an entirely tourist audience, and hence, we did tastings; what we ate is what we wrote. When it all came together, there it was, a quiet, defining essence on the footer: ‘With love from Baltistan’.
